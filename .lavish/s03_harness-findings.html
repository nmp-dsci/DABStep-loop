findings s03 · after plan s02 · 15 Sep 2026 · v2 on dev-10, three harnesses, eight runs

Adopt lean: 227k → 49k input tokens per question with the same score over three passes each; reject lean-prune, which re-opened outputs, added a call per question and lost three list answers on one pass

The experiment ran the champion's unchanged files under three harness profiles on the same ten questions. Removing the inherited connector tools and the title call cut the tokens per question by 78% and the per-question cost by 28%, and the ten tasks passed 24 of 30 times under both baseline and lean. Pruning tool output to a reference saved nothing over lean and cost accuracy, so it is not adopted. lean is now the default for every run and every loop session.

  • Fixed: agents/v2, Haiku 4.5, effort medium, 20 turns, dev-10, 3 workers, one pass per run.
  • Varied: the harness only; recorded in run.json.harness; the fingerprint 665f5ffa88eb is on every run.
  • Judged: per task across passes, not by p-value: no task that passed on every baseline pass may fail on every lean pass, and mean passes per run must not drop.

4 · why prune failed

4 · Pruning cut through 2k-character pandas prints, so the model re-queried instead of reading — more calls, more tokens than lean, and three wrong lists

What happened, from the traces

  • 34 outputs pruned over 20 tasks, 10 re-opened. Most pruned outputs were 2,000–2,100 characters: pandas' default display of a frame or a long list, just over the 1,500 cap.
  • Re-opening was the minority response. On 1464 (pass 1) six outputs were pruned, none re-opened; the model ran the query six ways, took 9 turns against 2 on baseline, and returned a longer, wrong list. On 1305 it re-opened five times and took 16 turns.
  • The answer was the output. 1464, 1681 and 1753 are "list every applicable fee id" tasks: the list is the long print. Hiding its tail hid the answer.
  • Net tokens rose against lean: 56.6k vs 49.1k per question (+15%), because each extra turn re-sends the conversation; the pruned characters were 10–15% of it.

What it means for the NVIDIA idea

  • The mechanism works — show() windows, references, full text kept in the trace — and it is content-blind, so it stays available as lean-prune.
  • It is the wrong lever here. Tool results are a small share of a short conversation once the prefix is gone; the model's re-query habit costs more than the saving.
  • If revisited: a cap of 3,000+ with a 1,500 head would leave pandas prints intact and prune only the manual.md reads (10k chars) — a few tasks, a small saving.
  • Not adopted; nothing to undo. The default is lean; HARNESS=lean-prune remains selectable for a future experiment.

6 · next lever

6 · After the harness, the cost is turns — and the flips are the answer contract, both agent questions

Recommended next

  • Answer-contract enforcement in the harness: 49 failed twice in these eight runs by answering in prose with no {"agent_answer"} line; a final message without it should be bounced back once ("reply with the JSON line only"). Harness-owned, content-blind, and the fix that v3/v4's holds pointed at.
  • Turn cost on the long tail: 1871 and 2697 are 58% of lean's tokens; output tokens are 44% of the dollar. The unsupervised loop's routing table is the mechanism for fewer calls; it is an optimiser cycle, not a setting.

Not worth another run

  • Prune cap tuning: a 3,000 cap would prune ~4 outputs across dev-10 — a saving under 5%.
  • Manual digest (T3): only 2697 read manual.md heavily; the tokens are there but so is the risk of a systematic wrong digest.
  • A cacheable prefix pad: padding the system prompt to Haiku's cache minimum would trade fresh tokens for cache writes at the same price; nothing to gain.
